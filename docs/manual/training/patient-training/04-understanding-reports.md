## Specialist Opinion Structure

Every specialist opinion on Dr. Bubal Care is structured in three sections. This format ensures you receive a clear, complete, and actionable report.

### Section 1: Clinical Findings

**What it is:** A detailed account of what the specialist observed in your records.

This section describes the specific findings from your slides, scans, lab results, or clinical notes. It is written in clinical language but focuses on factual observations — not interpretations or recommendations.

**Example:**

> **Clinical Findings:**
>
> The chest X-ray demonstrates a 1.8 cm solitary pulmonary nodule in the right lower lobe, adjacent to the minor fissure. The nodule appears solid with well-defined margins. No additional pulmonary nodules are identified. The heart size is within normal limits. No pleural effusion or pneumothorax is seen. The mediastinal contours are unremarkable.

In this example, the specialist describes exactly what they see — the size, location, and appearance of the nodule, plus what is normal. They do not yet say what it might mean.

### Section 2: Clinical Impression

**What it is:** The specialist's assessment and interpretation of the findings.

This is where the specialist synthesizes what they observed and provides their professional interpretation. It may include differential diagnoses (a list of possible explanations) ranked by likelihood.

**Example:**

> **Clinical Impression:**
>
> A 1.8 cm solid pulmonary nodule in the right lower lobe. Given the patient's age (52) and smoking history, the differential diagnosis includes:
>
> 1. **Primary lung malignancy** — moderate probability given nodule size and patient risk factors
> 2. **Granuloma** — possible, particularly if prior granulomatous disease
> 3. **Hamartoma** — less likely given solid appearance
>
> The nodule exceeds the threshold for recommended follow-up imaging per Fleischner Society guidelines.

In this example, the specialist provides their assessment — what the findings might mean and how confident they are. The differential diagnoses give you and your local doctor a framework for understanding the possibilities.

> **[PLACEHOLDER: screenshot]** — A specialist opinion showing the Clinical Impression section

### Section 3: Recommended Next Steps

**What it is:** Actionable suggestions for what to do next.

This section translates the specialist's findings and impression into concrete next steps. It may include additional tests, referrals, follow-up timelines, or treatment considerations.

**Example:**

> **Recommended Next Steps:**
>
> 1. **CT chest with contrast** — Recommended to further characterize the nodule and evaluate for lymphadenopathy
> 2. **Pulmonology referral** — Consultation with a pulmonologist for discussion of biopsy options if CT findings are suspicious
> 3. **Follow-up timeline** — CT scan within 4 weeks; if stable, repeat imaging at 3 and 6 months per Fleischner Society guidelines
> 4. **Smoking cessation** — Strongly recommended regardless of outcome

In this example, the specialist gives your local doctor a clear action plan — what test to do next, who to see, and when to follow up.

## Plain-Language Summary

Every specialist opinion on Dr. Bubal Care includes a **plain-language summary** — a patient-friendly translation of the clinical opinion written in everyday language. No medical jargon. No unexplained abbreviations. Written as if by a trusted GP.

The plain-language summary appears directly below the specialist opinion in your portal.

### Comparison: Opinion vs Plain-Language Summary

| Specialist Opinion (Clinical Language) | Plain-Language Summary (Everyday Language) |
|---------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------|
| "A 1.8 cm solid pulmonary nodule in the right lower lobe" | "The scan shows a small growth, about the size of a marble, in the lower part of your right lung" |
| "Differential diagnosis includes primary lung malignancy" | "One possibility your doctor will want to rule out is lung cancer" |
| "CT chest with contrast recommended within 4 weeks" | "You should have a more detailed scan of your chest within the next 4 weeks" |

The plain-language summary is **not a separate opinion** — it is a translation of the specialist's signed opinion into language you can understand and share with your family or local doctor.

## Panel Synthesis

If you chose **Panel Review** (2–4 specialists), you receive multiple independent opinions. When all specialists have submitted their signed opinions, the system automatically generates a **multi-opinion synthesis**.

The synthesis is an AI-generated draft that organizes the specialist opinions into three sections. Remember — **"AI surfaces; specialists decide."** The synthesis helps you understand the panel's collective view, but each opinion is independently signed by a human specialist.

### Areas of Agreement

This section lists the points where all or most specialists agree.

**Example:**

> **Areas of Agreement:**
>
> All four specialists agree that:
> - The pulmonary nodule requires further investigation with a CT scan
> - The patient's smoking history is a significant risk factor
> - Follow-up imaging within 4 weeks is appropriate
> - Smoking cessation should be strongly recommended

When specialists agree, you can be more confident in those conclusions because multiple experts independently reached the same view.

### Areas of Disagreement

This section highlights where specialist opinions differ — and how.

**Example:**

> **Areas of Disagreement:**
>
> - **Specialists A and B** assess the probability of malignancy as moderate, recommending CT-guided biopsy if the nodule persists
> - **Specialists C and D** assess the probability as lower, recommending initial CT surveillance before invasive procedures
> - All specialists agree the nodule needs investigation, but differ on how urgently biopsy should be considered

Disagreement is not a failure — it reflects the genuine complexity of clinical medicine. Seeing where experts differ helps you and your local doctor make a more informed decision.

### Consensus Summary

This is an overall synthesis of the panel's collective view, written in plain language.

**Example:**

> **Consensus Summary:**
>
> Your scan shows a small growth in your right lung that needs further investigation. All four specialists agree that a more detailed CT scan should be your next step, ideally within the next month. They also agree that stopping smoking is important regardless of the outcome. There is some difference of opinion on how quickly a biopsy should be considered — two specialists suggest it sooner, while two prefer to wait for the CT results first. Your local doctor can help you decide the best path based on this range of expert opinions.

## Practice Scenario

**Scenario:** You submitted a case for Specialist Opinion about an abnormal blood test result. Two specialists have now reviewed your case and submitted their opinions. The panel synthesis has been generated.

**Your task:**
1. Log in to `patient.drbubalcare.com` and open your completed case
2. Read the **Clinical Findings** section from Specialist A — identify what they observed in your lab results
3. Read the **Clinical Impression** section — note the differential diagnoses they listed
4. Read the **Recommended Next Steps** — write down the tests or referrals suggested
5. Read the **plain-language summary** — compare it to the specialist opinion and note how it translates the medical terms
6. Scroll to the **Panel Synthesis** section
7. Review the **Areas of Agreement** — note what both specialists agreed on
8. Review the **Areas of Disagreement** — note where their opinions differed
9. Read the **Consensus Summary** for the overall synthesis

**Expected outcome:** You can clearly explain what the specialists found, what they think it means, and what they recommend next — both in clinical terms and in plain language. You understand where they agreed and where they differed.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It's Wrong | What to Do Instead |
|---------|---------------|-------------------|
| Reading only the plain-language summary and skipping the specialist opinion | The specialist opinion contains clinical details that may be important for your local doctor | Read both — use the plain-language summary to understand the opinion, and share the full specialist opinion with your doctor |
| Ignoring the Recommended Next Steps section | This section contains the specific actions you and your doctor should take | Read the Recommended Next Steps carefully and share them with your local healthcare provider |
| Treating panel synthesis disagreement as a problem | Disagreement between specialists reflects clinical complexity, not a mistake | Review the Areas of Disagreement to understand the different perspectives, then discuss with your doctor |
| Assuming the AI-generated synthesis is the final clinical opinion | The synthesis is an AI-generated draft — every specialist's opinion is independently signed by a human | Each specialist opinion is the authoritative clinical document; the synthesis helps you compare and understand them |
| Not asking follow-up questions when something is unclear | The opinion may contain terms or recommendations you do not understand | Use the **"Ask a Follow-up Question"** feature — the AI assistant answers within the scope of the written opinion |

## Knowledge Check

1. **What are the three sections of a specialist opinion?**
   - A) Diagnosis, Treatment, Prognosis
   - B) Clinical Findings, Clinical Impression, Recommended Next Steps
   - C) Summary, Analysis, Recommendation
   - D) Observations, Interpretation, Referral
   - **Correct answer:** B — Every specialist opinion contains Clinical Findings (what was observed), Clinical Impression (the specialist's assessment), and Recommended Next Steps (actions to take next).

2. **What is the purpose of the plain-language summary?**
   - A) To replace the specialist opinion with a simpler version
   - B) To provide a second opinion from a different specialist
   - C) To translate the specialist opinion into everyday language without medical jargon
   - D) To summarize only the Recommended Next Steps section
   - **Correct answer:** C — The plain-language summary is a patient-friendly translation of the specialist's signed opinion. It is not a separate opinion or a replacement — it helps you understand the clinical language.

3. **What does the "Areas of Disagreement" section in a panel synthesis show?**
   - A) Where the AI disagrees with the specialist's opinion
   - B) Where patient information was incomplete or missing
   - C) Where specialists on the panel reached different conclusions or assessments
   - D) Where the specialist opinion contradicts your local doctor's findings
   - **Correct answer:** C — The Areas of Disagreement section highlights genuine differences in expert opinion. This reflects clinical complexity and helps you and your doctor make a more informed decision by understanding the range of expert perspectives.

## Summary

In this module, you learned:

- Every specialist opinion has three sections: **Clinical Findings**, **Clinical Impression**, and **Recommended Next Steps**
- **Clinical Findings** describe what the specialist observed — the facts from your records
- **Clinical Impression** is the specialist's assessment — what the findings might mean
- **Recommended Next Steps** are the concrete actions you and your doctor should take
- The **plain-language summary** translates the specialist opinion into everyday language for you and your family
- When you choose **Panel Review**, the system generates a **multi-opinion synthesis** with Areas of Agreement, Areas of Disagreement, and a Consensus Summary
- You can ask **follow-up questions** about your opinion using the AI assistant — it answers within the scope of the written opinion
- Every case closes with a human-signed opinion — **"AI surfaces; specialists decide"**
